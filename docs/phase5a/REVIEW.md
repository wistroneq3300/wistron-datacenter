## Why the previous exterior looked like a demonstration model

| Observed problem | Code / image cause | Bounded correction and how to judge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Windows appeared as attached plaques | Multiple flat window/frame layers sat in front of a solid wall. Side views exposed their protrusion rather than a deep opening. | Cut actual facade voids, put glass behind the stone reveal, give trim edges a small bevel. Check the entrance and grazing side view; adding AO alone cannot create missing geometry. |
| Blue-green glass read as a uniform plastic surface | Dark tint, relatively metallic material and a featureless LDR environment provided little reflection structure. | Use view-dependent environment reflection, lower the tint and keep glass/frame/slab separate. Inspect front and oblique views; a successful loader is not proof of reflective material quality. Generic HDRI is lighting content, not a surveyed Taipei backdrop. |
| Flat white stone lacked scale | Small repeating UVs were stretched across differently sized boxes; joints and surface variation did not establish one material scale. | Use metre-space UVs, restrained stone PBR normal/roughness and real aperture shadows. The visible source is grey granite-like cladding; no stone product or reflectance has been measured. |
| Weak contact and coarse shadow edges | A wide shadow frustum spread a 2048 map over roughly 110 m, approximately 54 mm per texel, with a relatively large normal bias. | Tighten the exterior light frustum; lower bias carefully; add bounded HQ-only contact AO. Check leaves, canopy underside, window sills and tree/ground contact for halos, peter-panning or acne. |
| Canopy and entrance were generic | The previous reference did not resolve the canopy/door geometry or the correct entrance bay. | Public 2025 entrance imagery supports a curved front canopy, radial ribs, wall lights, broad recessed door and rough brand stone below the curved vertical glass bay. Dimensions remain interpreted. |
| Whole-building proportions were wrong | The historic overview shows four upper main glass bands plus two stone base rhythms; the initial implementation repeated seven upper bands. | Correct the visibly supported rhythm and the right wing / second glazed pavilion. Do not use a Draft label to excuse a discrepancy visible in the reference. Unseen rear/roof details remain plain and unverified. |
| Low camera presets became elevated views | Studio's common `OrbitControls.maxPolarAngle = .485π` forced the eye above its target when the director handed control back. | HQ alone permits `.68π`; ground and conservative facade bounds constrain free exploration. Compare actual final camera coordinates with the authored preset, not merely the initial tween target. |
| Delayed HQ entry could steal the camera | Cancellation was recorded only after awaiting the return to HQ. An obsolete request could then start a new preset/timeline. | Capture the request token before the await, preserve camera lease sequence and ignore obsolete completion. Test cancellation, replacement and disposal while the return is unresolved. |

## Three.js r170 integration boundary

The application remains on the locally pinned Three.js revision 170. Postprocessing is isolated in `HQLighting.render()`: `RenderPass → SSAOPass → OutputPass`. The existing PA `RawShaderMaterial` and its authored environment/shadow pipeline render directly when HQ is hidden. HQ temporarily sets its exposure and restores the renderer setting in `finally`; Hall/Rack use their established environment and fog. Returning to HQ reactivates the selected day/dusk/night profile rather than overwriting it with daytime fog.

The r170 [OutputPass source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r170/examples/jsm/postprocessing/OutputPass.js) applies renderer tone mapping/output transfer. The output transform must occur once. The r170 [EffectComposer source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r170/examples/jsm/postprocessing/EffectComposer.js) owns two render targets; canvas antialiasing does not automatically antialias those targets. The HQ pipeline marks its terminal OutputPass `needsSwap=false` and sets the unused `renderTarget1.samples=0`, retaining one consistent MSAA scene buffer rather than alternating two large multisample allocations. Pixel ratio, target samples and final output still require an explicit budget.

Both SSAO and GTAO normal overrides need special care with alpha-cut leaf meshes. A solid override material can turn a sparse tree into opaque cards. HQ excludes vegetation and the sky from the architecture AO normal/depth override, retains actual leaf alpha/shadow treatment, and restores visibility in `finally`. The r170 [GTAOPass source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r170/examples/jsm/postprocessing/GTAOPass.js) is an evaluated alternative, not the installed pass; it would add its own normal/AO/denoise targets and lifecycle obligations. No additional AO package or major frontend framework is needed.

The [r170 MeshPhysicalMaterial documentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r170/docs/api/en/materials/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html) supports physical glass and recommends an environment map. Transmission is not automatically a visual improvement when no verified interior exists behind a wall; opaque reflective glazing can be an honest approximation. IOR, coating and transmission parameters in this demonstration are presentation choices, not Wistron material specifications. The [r170 RGBELoader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r170/examples/jsm/loaders/RGBELoader.js) provides compatible HDR ingestion without upgrading the renderer. Photo and HDR provenance are tracked in [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md).

## Camera, scene and resource ownership

- `CameraDirector` remains the sole arbitrator: orbit, semantic transition or timeline. `HQJourney` has no separate animation loop and checks the director sequence before retaining or cancelling its lease.
- HQ view selection, 360° orbit and entrance fly-in are distinct from the existing eight-depth semantic navigation. Wheel, pointer, Escape, free-explore change, ordinary `go()` and configuration changes cancel HQ playback. Its own hidden handoff calls the semantic Interior transition without cancelling the covering fade.
- The final facade approach is covered by an actual UI fade before the doorway; the scene switches to the existing conceptual Interior. This does not invent a real HQ corridor. Node-to-board navigation remains the existing P4 contract.
- Geometry, materials, textures, vegetation instance buffers, shadow targets, HDR PMREM targets and postprocessing passes have explicit owners. Disposed HQ instances reject late texture attachment; tests must exercise real GPU allocation and an immediate dispose while loaders are still pending.
- The first executed GPU lifecycle check found a real retained texture. The pinned r170 `SSAOPass.dispose()` omits its noise texture and SSAO material. The HQ wrapper explicitly disposes those resources after the upstream method; vendor bytes remain unchanged. The retained failed run is `evidence/attempt-1-phase5a-hq.json`; the final lifecycle JSON must demonstrate that the wrapper fix prevents accumulation.
- Selecting light profiles currently retains the loaded day/dusk/night PMREM targets for fast switching. This is a bounded three-profile cache, not lazy HDR loading. Count and lifecycle assertions must not describe it as loading one profile at a time.
- `renderer.info.memory` reports counts, not GPU-memory bytes. At ultrawide resolution, floating-point MSAA composer targets can cost substantially more memory than the scene textures suggest. Record target dimensions/formats/samples separately; any byte estimate must state assumptions. No WebGL count is a VRAM measurement.

## Comparable visual and engineering gates

| Gate | Evidence required | What does not qualify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source-backed massing | Full historic reference and new full-building view: four upper glass rhythms, two base rhythms, stepped left profile, curved entrance bay, extended right wing and far glass pavilion | Treating every thin horizontal glass mullion as a floor; inventing a rear window schedule |
| Material / depth improvement | Entrance and grazing facade crops where stone joints, recessed sill, frame and reflection are independently readable | Merely adding normal maps or reporting material count |
| Natural vegetation | Actual close-up foliage silhouettes, branch taper, bark, nonuniform tree crowns and plausible scale; distant LOD without popping | Leaf count or a vegetation unit-test PASS |
| Night composition | Local entrance/street light pools, readable dark unlit surfaces, no globally luminous pavement or crushed-black facade | Changing only the sky to blue |
| Camera equivalence | 3440×1440, DPR 1, exact old position/target/FOV retained in JSON, plus new authored views | Comparing a small old building with a large new building and attributing all difference to detail |
| Navigation | Native real-time orbit/fly-in, pause/resume, cancel before/after awaits, touch and ordinary journey transitions | Seeking a static timeline and calling it uninterrupted playback |
| Performance | 120 consecutive actual frames per authored view, FPS/P95, complete HQ pass draw calls/triangles, geometry/texture counts and GPU/runtime | Reporting only the last fullscreen pass or estimating FPS from triangle count |
| Lifecycle | Repeated warmed create/render/dispose and scene/light switches; late loader completion after disposal | Counting disposed JavaScript objects without GPU rendering |
| Existing platform | P1–P4 regression and PA Rack material/palette capture after HQ transitions | Assuming HQ-only filenames prove absence of side effects |

The four matched cameras retain the old `43°` FOV: front `[-3,17,62]`, side `[65,19,-9]`, quarter `[48,29,53]`, all looking at `[-3,13,-12]`; entrance `[15,4.5,13]` looking at `[4.7,2.1,-3.8]`. ## Executable evidence and remaining conditions

`tests/v5/hq-journey.test.mjs` covers the camera ownership and asynchronous cancellation contract with the real Three.js vector/camera classes. `tests/v5/hq-browser.cjs` exercises actual rendering/controls, matched captures, frame measurements, palette isolation, synthesized mobile touch and GPU lifecycle. `tests/v5/movie.cjs` records the actual canvas at 3440×1440, including the live doorway fade, and verifies decoded changing frames. Execution status belongs to their JSON/TAP evidence; syntax checking alone is not PASS for rendering.

The original user photograph, recent complete facade/roof/rear photographs, survey dimensions, window schedule and physical mobile GPU remain unavailable. Public photographs can support the visible design corrections; they cannot certify exact current dimensions, every window, unseen interiors or real landscaping. The HQ remains Draft even if its visual presentation improves substantially.

The recorded Impeccable detector artifact, `evidence/design-detector.json`, contains 102 findings, including inherited page-wide tiny captions, tracked eyebrow labels, gradients and existing contrast issues. It is not a clean accessibility certification and was not used to redesign the frozen P1–P4 interface. The new HQ Draft/status text was raised to at least 11 px and portrait-mobile buttons to 44 px. The small-height compact layout and broader inherited UI remain separate limitations. Visual judgement above is independent from the detector score.

The first mobile pinch probe hit the hero action area after orbit cancellation, causing page zoom instead of OrbitControls dolly. Its failure remains in `evidence/attempt-1-hq5-controls-diagnostic.json`. The corrected probe verifies `elementFromPoint` for all four touch positions before injecting the gesture, records those positions, and leaves the UI visible. The resulting canvas distance changed from approximately 64.08 m to 43.04 m in the short diagnostic. This demonstrates an operable canvas gesture; it does not certify that the mobile overlay composition is uncluttered.
